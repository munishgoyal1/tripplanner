**Current task: Diagnose Perf Sandbox Trip Switch**

Staying read-only. Here is the diagnosis to hand to the sandbox window.

## 1. Why the top of Details keeps the old trip

The hero photo/summary and the city chips are the only two things in the workspace that are **not** part of the atomic switch payload, and both actively prefer stale data.

**Hero** — DestinationOverview.tsx is explicitly designed to keep the previous destination on screen:

- comment: *"we keep the prior content visible while the new destination loads so the panel never blanks out mid-switch"*
- on a cache miss it does **not** clear `data`; it just sets `loading` and fetches (DestinationOverview.tsx)
- the only visual cue is `stale` → `opacity-70` (DestinationOverview.tsx)
- `.catch()` is a deliberate no-op: *"keep prior content on transient failure"*

That was a good nicety when a destination change meant "user flipped focus"; after the atomic-switch work it is the one panel that lies about which trip is loaded. It also has its own 30-min module cache and its own `GET /destination/overview` round-trip (api.ts) — `TripWorkspaceView` carries only `view`, `map`, `itinerary` (types.ts), so nothing seeds it.

**City chips** — `cities` lives in TripPanel.tsx and is only ever written by `DestinationGuide`'s `onCities` callback after `fetchPlaceGuide` resolves. `city` / `kind` / `query` also survive the switch, so a filter scoped to the old trip's city stays selected.

The screenshot confirms the split precisely: the amber banner already reads "Popular spots in Punjab (Chandigarh…)" — that comes from `ov.destination`, which *was* seeded atomically — while the hero and chips are still Tamil Nadu.

**Suggested fix (cheap, honest):**
- `DestinationOverview`: keep the `getCachedOverview` seed, but when the incoming `destination` differs from `data.destination`, `setData(null)` before fetching. The `stale` dimming branch then becomes dead and can go.
- `TripPanel`: reset `cities` / `city` / `kind` / `query` on `tripVersion` change (or key the guide subtree by trip id).
- Resist putting the overview into the switch payload — `build_destination_overview` (trip_view.py) does a Places `top_places` + prefetch + Tavily news, i.e. it would land the slowest call in the workspace directly on the critical path you are already trying to shorten.

## 2. Why the switch got slower

- `switch_trip` now builds three view-models sequentially inside one `asyncio.to_thread`, under the workspace-exclusive lock (trip_operations.py + api.py). Time-to-first-panel went from `max(view, map, itinerary)` (three parallel client fetches) to `view + map + itinerary`. Everything now lands together, but later.
- `/trips/switch` enqueues **no** background warm, unlike `trip_view_endpoint` which backgrounds `warm_guide` (api.py). So immediately after a switch, the guide *and* the overview both fetch cold — which is exactly the tail the owner is watching.
- Suggested: add `background: BackgroundTasks` to `trips_switch` and enqueue `warm_guide` plus a new `warm_destination_overview` for the newly active trip. That makes the hero a cache hit without lengthening the response.

## 3. Notifications — the channel you are already building fits

notices.ts + `StatusBar` are the right home; nothing publishes to them yet beyond the `action-error` conversions in your working tree. For the switch specifically:

- post `notify({ id: "trip-switch", tone: "progress", message: 'Switching to "<name>"…' })` in `TripSwitcher.choose()` **before** `await switchTrip(tripId)`
- resolve it in `App.handleSwitched` after `applyView` — same id, `tone: "success"`, `"Now planning <name>"` (auto-dismisses at 4s), or `tone: "error"` in the switcher's `catch`

Reusing one id keeps a single slot, and resolving in `App` rather than the switcher is what makes the notice span the whole operation instead of ending when the fetch returns.

## Side note while you are in there

`panelSeed` in App.tsx is never cleared. `consumedSeedRef` makes re-application safe within a mounted panel, but `handleNewTrip` / `handleStartNewTrip` leave the previous trip's seed in state — remounting the map or itinerary pane after starting a new trip would seed it from the old trip. Clearing `panelSeed` on trip-change (or keying it by trip id) closes that.
